During the Spring 2026 semester, I taught two sections of introductory electromagnetism and optics laboratory courses through a paid position as an undergraduate teaching assistant for the Department of Physics. I independently taught both courses, handling grading, office hours, and the like with complete autonomy. Through these courses, I developed substantial skills and experience in face-to-face physics instruction, and I worked to foster a collaborative environment where students would work in small groups to troubleshoot problems during the lab periods.

Teaching is quite important to me. I intend to become a physics instructor after completing my physics PhD.

I am a member and executive of Student-Led Academic Peer Study (SLAPS), which is a student organization at the University of Florida that offers free academic mentoring (tutoring) and support to students in introductory physics courses. Through this organization, I spent over 200 volunteer hours offering free physics instruction to struggling students, at one point developing a group that was joined by over 180 students in a Physics 1 course at the university. I now am the Director of Senior Mentors of SLAPS, where I coordinate the activities of the mentors of the organization to ensure the most efficient possible instructional delivery to our mentees.

Fun Stuff
======

I believe firmly in the free dissemination of knowledge. To this end, I have been writing open access physics textbooks across my time at the University of Florida. I have self-published an introductory classical mechanics textbook, which I've dubbed "Physics 1 Forever," on Zenodo.

* **J.N. Grossmann III**, "Physics 1 Forever," Zenodo. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19667306

My Physics 1 textbook, which I authored in its entirety by myself, covers Newtonian mechanics, encompassing linear and rotational dynamics and elements of continuum mechanics. This book chronicles a trajectory in introductory physics exactly as I learned it, and if even one person is able to look at this book and understand a concept or problem better, then it was worth creating. Additionally, I included bonus sections on special relativity, Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics, a brief discussion of quantum mechanics and the Schrödinger equation, and a computational exploration of the chaotic behavior in a double pendulum system.

There will be another. My next book, "Physics 2 Genesis," will cover classical electromagnetism, optics, and thermodynamics, alongside the topics explored in a modern physics course. The book will also encompass concepts from higher-level electromagnetism, quantum mechanics, and thermal physics courses. It will also be flatly of a much higher quality than its predecessor, as I have become better at instructional writing and at using LaTeX through the process of creating these books. 

I will publish an interim version of the next book once the classical electromagnetism and optics portion of the text is complete. (I should be able to publish this interim version soon.)

Of course, with the additional topics these books explore, their identities certainly no longer align perfectly with traditional Physics 1 and Physics 2 courses, instead charting a trajectory that significantly surpasses the content these courses normally cover. However, these books are a passion project of mine, and I want to encompass as much of the content of a standard physics major as I can. 

I learned how to use LaTeX to write these books, and I write these books in VS Code, which in its base form lacks any traditional grammar checks. I therefore have littered these books with typos and such, which I am constantly fixing. These books may not be perfect, but I believe they accomplish the task I set out to accomplish.

I have freely published my work with a Creative Commons copyright license that enables their free redistribution. I simply want to expand the availability of knowledge regarding physics, a subject that society often invokes and yet covers in a veil of mystique. In "Physics 1 Forever," I argue that "Physics is forever, and forever is right now." I believe the value of studying physics becomes more and more salient with each passing day as pseudoscience and artificial intelligence grip the planet, and I hope to do my part to spread the knowledge of physics, both through these textbooks and as a future physics instructor.
